A structured screening protocol for hiring staff in an AI-augmented environment, where polished application materials no longer prove authentic competency.

The goal is to identify staff who can manage logistics accurately, communicate clearly with physicians and faculty, work independently without constant hand-holding, and use AI as a tool without outsourcing judgment to it. This guide does not try to detect whether a cover letter was written with AI. It tests whether the candidate can think, verify, escalate, and adapt when placed in a realistic situation.

Scope: Built for CME Coordinator hiring at an academic medical center, but applicable across healthcare administration, higher education operations, grants coordination, accreditation support, and compliance-adjacent staff functions.

Why Hiring Has Changed

Generative AI has made polished applications the floor, not the ceiling. The problem is no longer sorting weak prose from strong prose. It is sorting authentic judgment from convincing performance of judgment.

The application problem

  • Applicants can generate CME-fluent cover letters using the job description itself.
  • Perfect wording is no longer strong evidence of ability.
  • CME roles require real-time communication of standards to faculty under deadline pressure.

The interview problem

  • AI-rehearsed responses can collapse when the scenario changes.
  • Confidence, structure, and vocabulary are weak signals by themselves.
  • Live adaptive evaluation is more reliable than AI detection.
Hiring implication: Push candidates off their prepared talk track early. The signal lives in how they adapt when facts shift.

Competency Framework

The framework separates routine AI use from compliance-sensitive AI judgment. A candidate can be comfortable using AI and still be unsafe for a role that touches accreditation or policy.

Logistics and follow-through

  • Tracks people, deadlines, and next steps.
  • Closes loops without prompting.
  • Escalates before small issues become institutional risks.

Faculty communication

  • Clear, concise, respectful, and firm.
  • Can write under time pressure.
  • Knows when to resolve and when to escalate.

Compliance judgment

  • Does not guess on policy questions.
  • Uses authoritative sources.
  • Documents uncertainty and escalation.

Thirty-Minute Interview Plan

Minutes 0 to 15

Ask about past work, ownership, logistics, and a realistic missing-disclosure scenario.
  • What did the candidate personally own?
  • How did they follow up?
  • How did they communicate with a senior stakeholder?

Minutes 15 to 30

Test AI literacy, artifact review, compliance instinct, attention to detail, and candidate questions.
  • What would they use AI for?
  • What would they never use AI for?
  • When would they verify or escalate?

Live Artifact Review Exercise

Give the candidate 90 seconds to review an AI-drafted email. Ask what they would change before sending it and why.

Subject: Disclosure Reminder


Dear Dr. Johnson,


I am writing to remind you that your disclosure form for the upcoming Grand Rounds session on March 12, 2025 has not yet been received. Per office process, faculty disclosures must be completed before the CME activity. Failure to submit may result in removal from the program. Please complete the form and return it to the CME office by February 26, 2025.


Sincerely,
[CME Office]

Date and year verification

A strong candidate checks the activity date, deadline logic, and year.

Tone problem

The escalation language is too punitive as initial outreach.

AI filler

Strong candidates remove generic phrasing that adds no operational value.

Governance Lens

Accreditation risk

AI-generated confidence can create institutional exposure if staff relay unverified guidance.

Data privacy

Confidential candidate, faculty, learner, or activity data cannot be pasted into public AI tools without approval.

Escalation standards

The desired behavior is not knowing every rule. It is knowing when uncertainty requires verification or escalation.

AI-Ready Hiring Framework for CME and Academic Operations

Project Lead · LSU Health New Orleans, Office of Medical Education · 2026

Developed and implemented a structured interview screening protocol for CME Coordinator recruitment in an AI-augmented hiring environment. The framework addresses a critical institutional gap: the breakdown of cover letter screening as a signal of authentic candidate competency when generative AI tools can produce CME-fluent application materials indistinguishable from expert-written prose.

The protocol moves beyond detection, which is unreliable, to live adaptive evaluation of judgment, verification behavior, communication quality, and AI governance instinct. It includes a tiered AI literacy assessment model, a live artifact review exercise with deliberate errors for candidates to identify, a compliance trap question, a candidate work sample, a seven-area scoring rubric with hard-stop rules, and an AI context note for interviewers.

  • Validated screening protocol applicable across multiple hiring cycles.
  • Three-tier AI literacy assessment framework aligned to role-specific risk profiles.
  • Live artifact review exercise with scored error taxonomy covering date accuracy, tone, and AI filler.
  • Governance framework documenting accreditation risk, data privacy, and escalation standards.
  • Behavioral marker reference for hiring committees to recognize AI-dependent candidates.
  • Action research proposal for iterative protocol refinement over three hiring cohorts.

# Federal Clinical AI Roadmap: Faculty Brief

**Source evidence excerpt:** A public faculty-facing briefing translating a federal clinical AI roadmap into practical questions about autonomy, payment, evidence, clinical responsibility, medical education and governance across independently operated clinical affiliates.

## Live artifact

[Read the Clinical AI Roadmap Faculty Brief](https://datadrivenmed.github.io/CMS-Superintelligence/)

## Professional summary

The briefing connects national clinical AI strategy with the working context of clinical faculty who practice and teach across LCMC Health, Ochsner Health and FMOLHS/OLOL. Its organizing question is what faculty should understand, verify or ask locally as policy, payment models and technical capabilities evolve.

The public page includes a policy-status table, a timeline, a five-level working autonomy ladder, payment illustrations, an evidence-appraisal table, practical checklists, a glossary and source links. The autonomy ladder is presented as an analytical aid rather than an official federal classification.

## Role and contribution

Ram Paragi prepared a faculty-facing policy interpretation and educational briefing. The portfolio contribution is the synthesis, analytic framing, visual explanation and translation into questions relevant to distributed academic practice. The briefing is not an official CMS publication and does not establish institutional policy or authorize AI use at an affiliate hospital.

## Analytic approach

- Distinguish the roadmap's ambitions from the status of related programs, pilots, proposals and implementation dates.
- Connect clinical autonomy with evidence, ongoing monitoring, clinical responsibility and local governance.
- Explain the payment incentives discussed in the brief through charts, withhold illustrations and co-management examples.
- Distinguish performance on written cases and simulated encounters from evidence of benefit in clinical practice.
- Separate affiliate authority over clinical tools, EHR access and workflows from medical-school responsibilities for education, supervision, research and faculty development.

## Public deliverable

The approximately ten-minute brief combines an executive summary with deeper explanations of the federal roadmap, ACCESS, TEMPO, ADVOCATE and related regulatory discussions. It places selected study results alongside their settings and limitations. Action checklists help faculty identify local approval pathways, ask billing and governance questions, frame expectations for trainees and recognize issues requiring further review. Checklist selections remain in the reader's browser.

## Strategic significance

The work demonstrates the ability to translate a fast-moving federal technology agenda into practical communication for academic medicine. It connects policy status, healthcare economics, empirical evidence, clinical workflows and institutional decision authority without treating a national technology direction as permission to deploy a tool locally.

## Evidence boundaries

This is an informational brief, not an institutional directive, clinical protocol, individualized billing recommendation or legal determination. Its inclusion documents a public communication artifact; it does not establish formal endorsement, adoption, measured use or patient impact. Time-sensitive regulatory and payment details should be checked against current primary sources and the relevant local governance, billing or legal office before operational use. The portfolio entry describes the briefing's scope and does not independently certify every factual claim in it.

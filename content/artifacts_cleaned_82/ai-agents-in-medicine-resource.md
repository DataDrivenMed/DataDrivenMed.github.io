## Summary

AI Agents in Medicine is a public educational and governance resource designed for academic health sciences faculty and staff. It explains how AI agents differ from ordinary chatbots, why agentic workflows matter for medicine, and how autonomy, tools, memory, retrieval, permissions, and human review change the risk profile of AI systems.

The artifact is positioned as an institutional literacy resource for faculty, staff, researchers, program leaders, administrators, and governance teams who need to evaluate emerging AI-agent claims without confusing technical capability with approved deployment.

## What the artifact includes

The resource organizes AI agents in a way that is useful for academic medicine:

- Foundational explanation of what an AI agent is
- Distinction between chatbot interaction and tool-using workflows
- Agent architecture concepts such as planning, tool use, retrieval, memory, orchestration, and human checkpoints
- Practical examples across documentation, research, medical education, clinical workflows, operations, and institutional knowledge support
- Risk and safety considerations including autonomy, cascading error, privacy, cybersecurity, accountability, and overreliance
- Governance framing for evaluating agentic tools before institutional adoption

## Institutional design logic

The resource addresses a major institutional challenge: AI-agent products are entering academic medicine faster than most organizations can create shared language, governance criteria, and evaluation workflows.

The artifact helps academic health sciences audiences ask the right questions before adoption:

- What is the agent allowed to access?
- What tools can it use?
- Can it retrieve, write, send, schedule, document, or modify anything?
- Where does human approval occur?
- What evidence supports performance claims?
- What happens when the system makes a multi-step error?
- Is the use case educational, research-facing, operational, clinical, or partner-governed?

It supports the portfolio's AI governance and digital transformation capability by showing:

- Alignment with medical education, research strategy, clinical partner pilots, and administrative operations

## Evidence boundary

This is a public education and planning resource. It does not authorize clinical agent deployment, PHI use, EHR integration, autonomous patient-facing actions, or partner health-system workflow adoption. Any clinical, EHR-connected, patient-facing, documentation, communication, revenue-cycle, or operational implementation would require institutional governance, partner approval, privacy review, security review, validation, and human oversight.

# Research Associate at OMERAD (2007-2010)

## Strategic Execution and Faculty Development
During my tenure at OMERAD in New Orleans, I drove the development, execution, and dissemination of critical educational research and scholarly work within the sphere of medical education. I took a hands-on approach to conducting and assisting with comprehensive faculty development programs. These initiatives were strategically designed to instill highly effective teaching and learning methodologies across the institution. Beyond standard pedagogical training, my work actively cultivated scholarship in teaching, enhanced faculty effectiveness as organizational leaders, and instilled a culture of continuous professional learning and reflective practice among medical educators. Furthermore, I served as a dedicated consultant to the Academy for the Advancement of Educational Scholarship. In this capacity, I supported a major health-sciences-center-wide initiative spearheaded by the Chancellor's Office, working to nurture an interdisciplinary community of educators and championing excellence in educational scholarship.

## Technical Execution and Assessment Design
I operated as a primary consultant for both faculty and administrative leadership regarding the complex assessment and evaluation of medical learners. I provided targeted, high-level advisement on the overarching design, deployment, and practical implementation of student outcome metrics. My technical focus centered heavily on creating rigorous, performance-based evaluation frameworks. This required deep expertise in integrating advanced, hands-on evaluative tools into the curriculum, including designing clinical simulations, managing simulated patient encounters, and leveraging computer-assisted learning and assessment platforms to ensure accurate measurement of student competencies.

## Institutional Impact and Outcomes
My work had a direct, structural impact on the institution's academic advancement pipelines. By collaborating extensively with cross-functional offices, departmental groups, and individual stakeholders, I led the identification, development, and continual refinement of sophisticated teaching assessments. The evaluation frameworks I established were crucial for facilitating the appropriate recognition of excellence in educator roles. Ultimately, the assessment models I refined served as the foundational metrics supporting critical institutional processes, including faculty promotion decisions, the acquisition of tenure, merit-based compensation evaluations, and the formal distribution of teaching and education awards.

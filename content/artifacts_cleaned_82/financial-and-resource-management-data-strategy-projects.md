# Assistant Professor and Director of Accreditation & Strategic Planning Compliance (2017-Present)

## Strategic Execution and Financial Management
Operating within the Office of Faculty and Institutional Affairs and the Dean's Office at the LSU School of Medicine in New Orleans, I engage in the continuous and ongoing analysis of Hospital CMS (Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services) Cost reports, complex hospital financing models, and Graduate Medical Education (GME) funding specifically tailored to the Louisiana Hospital Enterprise. I comprehensively examine financial data, cost structures, reimbursement mechanisms, and overarching regulatory compliance. I continuously monitor and evaluate diverse sources of hospital financing, encompassing government funding, private payer reimbursements, grants, and philanthropic contributions. By analyzing the ongoing funding mechanisms and allocations for GME programs—taking both federal and state funding sources into rigorous account—my work directly supports informed decision-making and empowers LSUSOM leadership to adapt and thrive in a dynamic healthcare environment. Furthermore, I support the rigorous verification and validation of LSU School of Medicine departmental-level funding distribution. Through deep data analysis of financial records, allocation documents, and budgetary information, I ensure strict accuracy, compliance, and optimal resource allocation across the institution.

## Technical Execution and Data Architecture
In a pivotal, highly technical role, I led the development of a comprehensive data reporting structure for the Managed Care Incentive Payment (MCIP) program, collaborating closely with informatics experts. To support this groundbreaking initiative, which harnessed federal regulations allowing State Medicaid programs to integrate incentive arrangements into Managed Care Organization (MCO) contracts, I designed a robust data architecture. I seamlessly integrated advanced data analytics and informatics methodologies while implementing rigorous data quality assurance measures to create a highly structured reporting framework. This resulted in a system that not only strictly complied with federal regulations but also provided actionable, data-driven insights to optimize incentive strategies, ultimately enhancing healthcare outcomes and cost management within Medicaid Managed Care. Additionally, I conducted a detailed comparative analysis of National Institutes of Health (NIH) grant funding, tailored specifically to the needs of the Dean. In this report, I benchmarked our institution's grant acquisition performance against peer institutions, systematically identifying structural strengths, weaknesses, and key opportunities for competitive improvement.

## Impact, Grants, and Healthcare Outcomes
My financial and resource management analysis has a profound impact on institutional funding acquisition and statewide healthcare policy. I conducted a comprehensive analysis covering state GME Medicaid funding, Disproportionate Share Hospital (DSH) expenditures, and LSU Hospital Medicaid funding, meticulously preparing this data for the Department of Health and Hospitals (DHH) Secretary's high-level reporting and presentations. By incorporating statistics on Louisiana's State Primary Care physician workforce, I provided the Secretary with nuanced, holistic insights crucial for policy formulation, strategic planning, and legislative advocacy. 

I am heavily involved in driving empirical evidence for targeted grant funding. I provided vital data-driven insights for LSUHSC alumni applying for the Board of Regents Support Fund (BoRSF) Endowed Superior Graduate Student Scholarships Subprogram in collaboration with the LSU Foundation. I also compiled and analyzed critical data on physician workforce shortage areas to build a compelling case for a separate LSU Foundation grant aimed at enhancing state healthcare delivery. To support the establishment of the TexLa Telehealth Resource Center (TLTRC), I gathered and analyzed meticulous workforce data to demonstrate the pressing need to extend telehealth resources to underserved regions of Texas and Louisiana. Finally, to advance LSU's strategic and equity plan, I conducted a focused analysis for the Associate Vice Chancellor for Academic and Multicultural Affairs regarding medical school graduates currently practicing in Medically Underserved Areas/Populations (MUA/P). By evaluating geographic distribution, specialty choices, and duration of service, I provided the foundational insights necessary to shape strategic initiatives designed to increase healthcare access and inform future academic planning.

# Generative AI Use-Case Atlas for Academic Health Sciences

## Evidence status

Live public artifact. Faculty and staff education artifact. AI governance and implementation planning resource. Non-PHI public demonstration.

Live artifact: https://genai-healthcare-usecase-atlas.vercel.app/

## Summary

The Generative AI Use-Case Atlas for Academic Health Sciences is an interactive decision-support and faculty education artifact designed for university health sciences environments. It organizes practical generative AI opportunities across teaching, clinical care, biomedical research, research administration, accreditation, operations, and partner-governed health-system workflows.

The project was developed for an academic medical center context where responsible AI adoption requires more than a list of tools. Faculty and staff need to understand what AI can support, which models and platforms may be relevant, what data sensitivity is involved, when partner approval is required, and how governance, human review, HIPAA, IRB, and clinical validation requirements change across use cases.

## What the artifact includes

The atlas includes more than 60 structured use-case cards across four major domains:

- Teaching & Education
- Clinical Care
- Research & NCI Readiness
- Administration & Operations

Each use case is labeled by priority, data sensitivity, implementation authority, partner involvement, NCI relevance, recommended models, governance considerations, sample prompts, metrics, and source links.

The site also includes a model and tool directory, prompt library, governance section, partner-governed clinical AI pilot framework, source library, and filters by domain, risk, implementation authority, partner involvement, and NCI relevance.

## Institutional design logic

A major design feature is the distinction between institution-led and partner-governed implementation.

Education, faculty development, research support, accreditation, operations, and non-PHI workflows can often be led internally. EHR-connected workflows, patient communication workflows, ambient documentation, revenue-cycle workflows, clinical outreach, and health-system operations require appropriate partner health-system approval, privacy review, and operational governance.

This distinction is important for an academic health sciences center that trains learners and conducts research across affiliated clinical sites rather than directly owning hospitals.

## Research and NCI readiness

The research section was intentionally expanded to support biomedical research strategy and cancer-center readiness. It includes use cases related to grant intelligence, NIH/NCI portfolio analytics, clinical-trial feasibility, cancer imaging AI, computational pathology, genomics and multi-omics interpretation, catchment-area analytics, population science, shared resource utilization, and research compliance support.

This makes the artifact relevant not only as an AI literacy resource, but also as a research infrastructure and NCI-readiness planning tool.

## Technical implementation

The artifact was built as a static, portfolio-ready web application using Next.js, React, Tailwind CSS, TypeScript data structures, shadcn/ui-style components, Lucide icons, GitHub source control, and Vercel deployment.

The static deployment model makes it suitable for public portfolio use, faculty and staff education, and future expansion.

## Strategic value

This artifact demonstrates how generative AI can be translated from a broad technology trend into a practical institutional framework for academic medicine.

It helps faculty, staff, researchers, administrators, program leaders, and governance teams answer five core questions:

1. What can generative AI support in an academic health sciences center?
2. Which use cases are safe to start now with non-PHI content?
3. Which use cases require partner hospital approval or clinical governance?
4. Which models, tools, and platforms should be evaluated for each workflow?
5. What governance, evidence, and human review steps are needed before implementation?

## Evidence boundary

This is a public educational and planning artifact. It does not approve clinical deployment, PHI use, EHR integration, or health-system operational adoption. Any clinical, EHR-connected, revenue-cycle, documentation, communication, or outreach workflow would require appropriate partner approval, privacy review, institutional governance, and human oversight.

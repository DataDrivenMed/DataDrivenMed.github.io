# Program Development and Medical Education Leadership

## Strategic Execution and Program Development
I played a pivotal role in the strategic development and successful implementation of several key healthcare programs aimed at expanding medical services and enhancing clinical training. For the LSU School of Medicine, I was instrumental in establishing The Enhancing Quality Improvement for Patients (EQuIP) Program. This institution-wide initiative was designed to actively engage residents and fellows in comprehensive, systems-based quality improvement endeavors. In this capacity, I contributed significantly to shaping the overarching program structure, establishing robust data collection methodologies, evaluating and determining priority areas for improvement, selecting performance measures strictly aligned with our goals, and instituting structural mechanisms for continuous performance monitoring. 

Furthermore, I performed an in-depth Graduate Medical Education (GME) data analysis to directly support the development and creation of a new Family Medicine Residency Program at Bogalusa. Designed to aid the Chair of the LSU SOM's Family Medicine Department, this comprehensive study evaluated key indicators such as regional healthcare needs, potential patient demographics, and existing educational infrastructure. These data-driven insights were instrumental in shaping the residency program's structure, curriculum, and strategic partnerships. Additionally, I served as an official Ambassador for the National Health Service Corps (NHSC), providing dedicated consultation to students regarding available loan repayment programs through the NHSC and the state. I educated students about the valuable opportunities to serve in underserved areas in exchange for these repayment benefits.

## Technical Execution and Healthcare Finance Analytics
To support complex institutional financing and healthcare accessibility, I conducted exhaustive financial data analyses across multiple state contexts. I provided vital evaluation and consultation for a comprehensive Medicaid Section 1115 waiver proposal designed to address the healthcare needs of underserved populations while simultaneously promoting physician workforce development. I analyzed a key component of this waiver that proposed restoring Medicaid funding by strategically directing GME support toward a community-based program, specifically envisaging the establishment of a teaching health center for the Family Medicine program. Through thorough analysis, I rigorously assessed the potential impact and feasibility of this proposal, contributing to a deeper understanding of its implications on healthcare accessibility and community-based physician training. 

I also conducted an in-depth analysis of Hospital CMS (Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services) Cost reports, hospital financing, and GME funding with a specific focus on the Texas context. This analysis provided UTMB leadership with valuable insights into their operations, enabling data-driven decisions to enhance healthcare delivery and medical education within the Texas landscape. Expanding this financial analysis to Louisiana, I executed an in-depth evaluation of CMS Medicare funding for the LSU System Office, focusing heavily on Medicare GME funding caps. This comprehensive report broke down the deep complexities of Medicare funding mechanisms, offering a clear, empirical picture of how financial resources were being allocated across various medical institutions in the state.

## Impact, Research, and Educational Outcomes
My programmatic and analytical efforts left a lasting impact on healthcare quality, institutional funding, and educational methodologies. The data and insights I gathered through the EQuIP program played a critical role in driving evidence-based decision-making and strategic planning for ongoing quality improvement efforts, effectively embedding a commitment to patient care and safety into the institution's culture. 

In the realm of emergency preparedness, I developed a robust data analysis and research plan focused on Emergency Medicine (EM) simulation disaster training. By outlining key objectives to evaluate current training modules, assess skill acquisition, and measure real-world disaster response impacts utilizing both quantitative metrics and qualitative feedback, I created a structured roadmap for continual programmatic improvement. These insights directly informed curriculum enhancements, guided strategic resource allocation, and contributed broadly to the field of emergency medicine education by ensuring the training aligned with best practices. Finally, I provided crucial data support that led to securing a Health Resources and Services Administration (HRSA) grant aimed at enhancing the Department of Family Medicine. To achieve this, I collaborated extensively with interdisciplinary teams to compile vital data and draft compelling, evidence-backed narratives.

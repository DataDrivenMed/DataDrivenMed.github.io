# HumanSim — Living Visual Medical Knowledge Network

## Professional summary

HumanSim is a public formative medical-education prototype designed around one computational patient rather than separate specialty or discipline modules. The core educational problem is fragmentation: students often learn anatomy, physiology, biochemistry, pathology, pharmacology, diagnostics, and clinical medicine as separate bodies of knowledge and may struggle to connect them when a patient changes. HumanSim is designed to make those relationships visible and traversable from the same patient state.

## Role description

Concept owner and product designer. Defined the educational model, simulation architecture, connected-knowledge approach, learner workflow, and validation boundaries, and directed iterative implementation of the public prototype. The design intentionally separates the patient-state/simulation layer from the explanatory AI layer so the system does not treat free-form language generation as authoritative physiology.

## Accomplishments

- Reframed the experience from an anatomy-first floating human into a patient-first learning workflow: enter a patient, investigate or intervene, observe the response, open WHY, compare mechanisms, move through time, explore connections, and replay.
- Integrated a detailed BodyParts3D anatomical atlas with the HumanOS patient-state engine so anatomy remains the spatial anchor while the mechanism becomes the curriculum.
- Designed a full-screen WHY workspace with Mechanism, Compare, Time, and Connections views and Core, Deep, Clinical, and Board learning depths.
- Structured learning around reference normal, disease disturbance, compensatory physiology, intervention, and response over time rather than isolated facts.
- Added concept resolution and curated mechanism dossiers so a recognized medical concept can still be learned even when a quantitative HumanOS intervention model is not yet available.
- Designed the knowledge model to support bidirectional travel from molecule to patient and from patient back to molecule, with context determining which relationships are most relevant.
- Preserved explicit evidence boundaries: the public build is a formative educational prototype, not a validated clinical decision-support system, not a validated all-specialty physiologic simulator, and not represented as an institutionally adopted curriculum platform.

## Strategic significance

HumanSim extends a long-standing simulation, assessment, and medical-education portfolio into a digital learning system focused on connectedness. The design question is not simply whether a learner can name a diagnosis or intervention, but whether the learner can explain why the patient is changing across organ, tissue, cellular, molecular, physiologic, pathologic, pharmacologic, diagnostic, and clinical levels.

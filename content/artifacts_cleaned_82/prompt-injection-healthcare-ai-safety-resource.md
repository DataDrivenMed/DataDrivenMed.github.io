## Summary

Prompt Injection in Healthcare is a public AI safety and governance resource designed for academic health sciences faculty and staff. It explains prompt injection as a trust, workflow, and system-design problem rather than as a simple bad-prompt issue.

The resource is built for practical institutional use. It translates a technically complex AI failure mode into role-based guidance for clinicians, educators, researchers, administrators, privacy teams, compliance teams, informatics leaders, and AI governance committees.

## What the artifact includes

The artifact includes plain-language explanation, healthcare-specific examples, a static interactive simulator, warning signs, user precautions, response steps, organizational controls, and the PAUSE framework.

PAUSE organizes risk review around:

- Permissions
- Approved tool and account
- Untrusted content
- Sensitive or strategic information
- External action and exposure

The resource also distinguishes prompt injection from related but different risks such as hallucination, phishing, malware, voluntary disclosure, vendor data-retention issues, excessive agency, and unsafe tool permissions.

## Institutional design logic

The resource addresses a central problem in academic health sciences AI adoption: faculty and staff increasingly use AI tools to summarize documents, interpret clinical or educational content, review research materials, draft communications, or interact with institutional knowledge systems. These workflows often involve content created outside the immediate trust boundary.

The artifact makes that risk understandable without overstating it. It emphasizes layered safeguards, approved tools, least privilege, task-scoped retrieval, human review, and escalation through institutional IT, privacy, compliance, security, informatics, or AI governance channels.

## Strategic value

This artifact demonstrates the ability to convert a technical AI-security issue into an educational and governance framework usable by non-technical academic health sciences audiences.

It strengthens the portfolio's AI governance capability by showing:

- Practical workforce education
- Healthcare-specific AI safety translation
- Cybersecurity-aware AI governance
- Risk communication for faculty and staff
- Operational safeguards for document, email, image, portal, research, and institutional knowledge workflows
- Awareness that ordinary users cannot compensate for weak system architecture

## Evidence boundary

This is an educational and governance planning resource. It is not an institutional policy, security standard, technical control, incident-response protocol, or authorization to use AI with PHI or confidential institutional data. Local institutional policy, partner-site requirements, privacy review, security review, and human oversight remain required for sensitive workflows.

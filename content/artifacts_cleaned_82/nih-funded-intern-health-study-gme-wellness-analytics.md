INTERN HEALTH STUDY
Multi-Institutional Research Collaboration: GME Wellness Data Analytics
Data Analyst & Institutional Liaison, NIH-Funded Intern Health Study 
(2024-Present)
Serve as data coordinator for LSU Health Sciences Center's 
  participation in University of Michigan's NIH-funded Intern Health Study, 
  a national longitudinal cohort study assessing stress, depression, and 
  wellness factors across 1,500-3,000 medical interns annually at 
  institutions nationwide

• Obtained and analyzed institution-specific longitudinal wellness data for 
  LSU Health interns, including:
  - Daily mood tracking and psychological symptom inventories
  - Stress assessments and environmental/programmatic factors
  - Depression risk factors and early warning indicators
  - Multi-modal data sources (surveys, wearable devices, genomic data)

• Applied wellness analytics to GME strategic planning initiatives, 
  identifying:
  - Institutional trends in intern stress patterns and response
  - Depression risk factors specific to LSU Health training environment
  - Program-level variables affecting resident well-being
  - Evidence-based intervention opportunities

• Developed data-driven recommendations for GME leadership to enhance:
  - Wellness infrastructure and support systems
  - Early intervention protocols for at-risk residents
  - Mental health resources aligned with ACGME requirements
  - Burnout prevention strategies

• Maintained strict confidentiality protocols and data security standards 
  for sensitive mental health information, ensuring:
  - HIPAA compliance for protected health information
  - Research participant privacy protection
  - Institutional Review Board (IRB) ethical standards
  - Data governance best practices

• Facilitated institutional engagement with national research consortium 
  studying psychological, genetic, and environmental determinants of 
  depression under high-stress conditions, led by University of Michigan's 
  Eisenberg Family Depression Center (PI: Srijan Sen, MD, PhD)

• Impact: Provided evidence base for institutional wellness initiatives 
  addressing burnout prevention and mental health support in residency 
  training, directly supporting LCME accreditation compliance (Elements 
  12.3-12.4: Personal Counseling/Wellness and Student Access to Health 
  Services) and GME program quality improvement in the nation's highest-
  priority area for graduate medical education

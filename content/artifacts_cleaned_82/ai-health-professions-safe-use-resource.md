## Summary

Safe Use of AI in Health Professions Education is a public faculty and staff education resource focused on responsible use of AI in health professions learning environments. It is designed for academic health sciences audiences who need practical guidance on what AI can support, where its limits matter, and how educational, privacy, assessment, and professionalism concerns should shape use.

The artifact positions safe AI use as an educational governance problem, not only a technology problem. It gives faculty, staff, learners, and program leaders a common framework for distinguishing appropriate learning support from unsafe, unreviewed, or policy-sensitive use.

## What the artifact includes

The resource supports practical AI literacy and safe-use decision-making across health professions education. It is aligned to concerns such as:

- Faculty and learner AI literacy
- Human review of AI-assisted work
- Use of non-PHI and synthetic content when possible
- Avoidance of unapproved tools for sensitive data
- Appropriate boundaries for assessment, grading, advising, documentation, and clinical learning
- Distinguishing education support from clinical decision-making
- Reinforcing institutional and partner-site policy requirements

## Institutional design logic

Health professions education has a distinctive AI governance problem. Learners, faculty, and staff may use AI for study support, teaching materials, assessment preparation, feedback, writing support, simulation, case generation, and clinical reasoning practice. These uses can be valuable, but they can also create risks involving accuracy, privacy, bias, professionalism, academic integrity, assessment validity, and inappropriate clinical reliance.

This artifact helps academic health sciences audiences frame safe use around role, data type, intended purpose, review responsibility, and institutional approval.

## Strategic value

It strengthens the portfolio by showing:

- Faculty and staff education design

- Alignment between AI adoption, accreditation expectations, professionalism, learner assessment, and clinical-site governance

## Evidence boundary

This is an educational and governance planning resource. It is not a formal institutional policy, course policy, student handbook provision, clinical standard, or authorization to use AI with PHI, student records, confidential assessment material, or partner health-system data. Local institutional policy, course requirements, clinical-site rules, privacy review, and faculty oversight remain required.

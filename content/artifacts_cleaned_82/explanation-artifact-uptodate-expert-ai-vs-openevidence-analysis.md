## **Explanation Artifact: UpToDate Expert AI vs. OpenEvidence Analysis**

**What is this** I built a comprehensive analytical framework and faculty report evaluating UpToDate Expert AI against existing clinical decision support tools like OpenEvidence. It serves as a practical guide for medical faculty and trainees, defining the appropriate clinical workflows, inherent risks, and specific use cases for deploying these AI systems at the point of care.

**Why this approach** Rather than treating clinical AI as a monolith, I evaluated the strict tradeoffs between competing systems based on real-world workflow. I determined that UTD Expert AI is necessary for complex, multi-factor clinical questions because it provides institutional accountability—every answer traces back to a named physician author and review date. However, I deliberately highlighted where OpenEvidence remains superior: point-of-care speed, free access for NPI-verified physicians, and a format that is less likely to induce blind trust in fast-paced environments.

**What would break** The most fragile point in this ecosystem is the unverified accuracy of UTD Expert AI in complex clinical scenarios. Wolters Kluwer has not published independent, head-to-head benchmarks for these multi-factor edge cases. If a provider relies entirely on the system for a patient with highly specific, overlapping comorbidities, the lack of transparent accuracy data means the tool's reasoning could fail without the user immediately realizing it.

**What I learned** The most critical discovery was realizing how the presentation layer of an AI tool dictates clinical risk. Because UTD Expert AI delivers answers in highly confident, citation-backed prose, residents are dangerously more prone to automation bias and likely to accept the output without independent verification. This shifted my core recommendation: until we have concrete accuracy data, attendings must actively supervise and verify any management decisions that trainees inform via this tool.

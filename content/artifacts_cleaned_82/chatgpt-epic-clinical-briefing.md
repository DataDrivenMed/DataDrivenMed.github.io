# ChatGPT + Epic: What It Means for Clinicians

## Professional summary

A public clinician-facing briefing explaining what it actually means when ChatGPT for Healthcare can connect to authorized patient information in Epic. The analysis focuses on the architecture and governance questions behind the announcement: what information may be accessible, how authorization works, what HIPAA and a business associate agreement do and do not permit, where the capability may help, what still requires clinician judgment, and why local health-system governance remains decisive.

## Role description

Author and analyst. Developed an approximately eight-minute clinical explanation intended for faculty and other clinical stakeholders who needed more than a technology headline. Framed the issue around practical clinical workflow, privacy, accountability, and distributed health-system governance rather than treating technical connectivity as equivalent to clinical approval.

## Accomplishments

- Explained the difference between authorized EHR connectivity and unrestricted access to a patient's chart.
- Translated HIPAA and business associate agreement concepts into practical clinician-facing boundaries without treating legal permissibility as proof of clinical appropriateness.
- Identified potential clinical uses while preserving the need for clinician review, local validation, workflow controls, and health-system approval.
- Emphasized the relevance of a distributed academic medical school model in which faculty practice and teach across separately governed partner health systems with different EHR configurations, AI capabilities, approvals, and operating rules.
- Explicitly separated national product capability from local deployment or endorsement; no claim is made that ChatGPT for Healthcare is approved, deployed, or available at LSU Health or any LSU clinical partner.
- Used the announcement as a practical example of responsible clinical AI translation: architecture, data access, privacy, workflow, human oversight, and governance must be understood together.

## Strategic significance

The briefing demonstrates the ability to convert a rapidly moving clinical AI development into a decision-ready explanation for clinicians and academic-health-system leaders. It sits within a broader responsible-AI portfolio that distinguishes technical capability from institutional readiness, authorized use, validation, and accountability.

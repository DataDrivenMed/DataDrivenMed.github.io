How to read this explorer

2026 report · FYE 2023 data

This map compares the community investment counted by the Lown Institute with the estimated value of hospitals’ tax exemptions. A negative balance is a deficit; a positive balance is a surplus.

IN PLAIN LANGUAGE

What does a hospital deficit actually mean?

A deficit means the hospital invested less in the community, under Lown’s definition, than the estimated value of its tax benefits. It can still be investing substantial amounts.

In this example, the hospital invested $6 million in the categories Lown counts, but that was $4 million below its estimated $10 million in tax benefits.

Illustrative example, not an actual hospital
MeasureAmount
Estimated tax benefits$10 million

$6 million invested − $10 million in tax benefits = −$4 million

Lown counts financial assistance, subsidized health services and certain community programs. It excludes Medicaid shortfall, medical education and research. Read “deficit” as a gap under this framework. The label alone does not establish that a hospital provides no community benefit, is losing money or has violated tax-exemption requirements.

  1. 1

    Read the colors

    The starting map shows the percentage of included hospitals with a deficit. Darker red means a larger share. Use Color states by to change the measure, and check its legend each time.

  2. 2

    Select a state

    Click the map or use the state menu. The summary cards, selection panel and hospital table update together. Choose All states for the national view, or Reset view to clear filters too.

  3. 3

    Look at individual hospitals

    Search by hospital or city, filter by deficit or surplus, and click a hospital for exact dollars. These search and status filters affect the table and its CSV download, while the map keeps its full state totals.

  4. 4

    Compare like with like

    Percentages describe the included hospitals; dollar totals also reflect hospital size and the number studied. Use the state rankings to compare the same measure. Neither measure is adjusted for population or patient need.

Total deficits and net balance answer different questions. If a state has $100 million in hospital deficits and $70 million in surpluses, its total deficits are $100 million, while its net fair share balance is −$30 million.
A quick guide to the five map measures
Map measureHow to interpret it
Hospitals with a deficit (%)Deficit hospitals divided by all included hospitals in that state. Higher means deficits are more common.
Total hospital deficits ($)The magnitude of negative hospital balances only. The map shows the gap as a positive amount; cards and exports retain the negative sign.
Net fair share spending ($)All deficits and surpluses combined. Red means a net deficit; green means a net surplus.
Investment / tax exemption (%)Total counted investment divided by total estimated tax exemption. 100% means equal amounts; above 100% means investment is greater.
Tax exemption value ($)The total estimated tax benefits for included hospitals. A larger amount does not by itself indicate better or worse performance.

The figures are a historical snapshot of a selected nonprofit hospital cohort, not every US hospital. “Community investment” follows Lown’s definition and excludes Medicaid shortfall, education and research. SOURCE & INTERPRETATION

Read the map in context.

An independent interactive view of the Lown Institute’s 2026 Fair Share Spending report ↗. Data snapshot: November 17, 2026. Financial period: FYE 2023.

Fair share spending follows Lown’s definition, not a legal spending requirement or a clinical quality rating.

What the measures mean

Deficits sum only negative hospital balances. Net spending combines deficits and surpluses. Deficit percentage uses hospital counts. Coverage divides total investment by total tax exemption.

What Lown includes and excludes

Investment includes assistance, subsidized services and community programs. Lown excludes Medicaid shortfall, education and research. Some system spending is allocated to individual hospitals.

Reconciliation and limitations

Calculated from 3,037 published hospital rows. Missouri has 63 rows, versus 64 in Lown’s summary. Ohio’s deficits sum to −$1,349,745,390, versus −$1,349M in that summary. The hospital rows yield 51.5% with deficits, versus the report’s 52% headline. Original hospital balances are retained; rounded components may differ by $1. This is a selected nonprofit cohort, not every US hospital.

Boundaries: PublicaMundi US states. All financial values are US dollars.

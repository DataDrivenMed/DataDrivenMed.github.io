Overview

Why this guide exists

The hiring environment at academic medical centers changed faster than most hiring processes. This tool helps distinguish real judgment from AI-polished performance.

Generative AI tools such as ChatGPT, Claude, Microsoft Copilot, and Grok can produce polished cover letters and rehearsed answers in seconds. Traditional screening methods were not built for this.

The problem is not AI use. The problem is AI substitution, candidates who cannot think, communicate, verify, or solve problems when AI is unavailable or inappropriate.

What has changed

Written materials are weaker signals. Screening on prose quality now selects partly for AI access and prompt quality.

Rehearsed answers are weaker signals. The real signal is how a candidate adapts when you add a constraint they did not prepare for.

AI literacy is now a baseline skill. Hiring managers should evaluate whether candidates can use AI responsibly while protecting judgment, accuracy, and confidentiality.

AI as thinking accelerator: hire this

  • Uses AI to draft, organize, compare, and revise
  • Explains decisions without the tool
  • Verifies dates, policies, facts, and tone
  • Adapts when a scenario changes

AI as thinking replacement: risk

  • Pastes output without review
  • Cannot elaborate when questioned
  • Treats AI as an authority on policy
  • Only produces polished outputs with no evidence of thinking
Overview

AI tools: responsible use in hiring

Use AI to support preparation, not to make selection decisions.

Do not input candidate names, detailed employment records, health information, student records, or other sensitive material into consumer AI tools. Use only approved enterprise systems for protected data.
ToolAppropriate useDo not use for
ClaudeLong-form drafting, interview guides, rubric languageScoring or ranking candidates
Microsoft CopilotEnterprise drafting, approved-note summaries, job descriptionsAutomated selection or unsupported data use
ChatGPTQuestion brainstorming, scenario generation, communication templatesProcessing sensitive candidate information

Screening

Application review

Screen for evidence, not prose polish.

Look for

  • Specific programs, systems, timelines, or stakeholders
  • First-person ownership: I tracked, followed up, reconciled, escalated
  • Relevant academic, clinical, research, or regulated context
  • Claims that can be probed live

Deprioritize

  • Generic excellence language
  • Long cover letters that repeat the posting
  • Perfect prose with no examples
  • Claims that sound impressive but cannot be verified live

Flag one claim to probe

Screening

Phone screen questions

A 10 to 15 minute screen for specificity, ownership, communication, and basic judgment.

Genuine experience deepens under questioning. AI-generated experience often becomes vague, contradictory, or overgeneralized.

Use the same structure for every candidate in the same role.

Evaluation

Green and red flags

Green flags

  • Specific examples without sounding rehearsed
  • Explains personal responsibility
  • Asks clarifying questions
  • Admits uncertainty and names a verification path
  • Treats AI as drafting support, not a decision-maker

Red flags

  • Cannot explain a cover-letter claim
  • Uses polished language with no examples
  • Says they would ask AI for policy answers
  • Catches nothing in the AI artifact exercise
  • Cannot adapt when the scenario changes
